Medicare open enrollment runs through December 7, and this year patients are choosing plans without one key number.
CMS has published the 2027 Part D figures but has not yet released the 2027 Part B premium and deductible, which
usually arrive in mid-November. Here is what is official, what is a projection, and what you can decide now.

## Official: Part D for 2027

- **Out-of-pocket cap on covered prescriptions: $2,400** for the year. Once you reach it, covered drugs cost nothing
  for the rest of 2027.
- **Standard Part D deductible: $700.**

For patients on insulin, inhalers, anticoagulants or newer diabetes and heart medications, the cap is the most important
number in Medicare. It converts an open-ended risk into a known ceiling.

## Projected, not official: Part B for 2027

The 2026 Medicare Trustees Report projects a standard Part B premium of about $209.50 a month, up roughly $6.60 from
$202.90 in 2026, and a Part B deductible near $292, up from $283. Treat these as estimates. Last year's trustees had
projected a higher 2027 figure, so the final number can move in either direction. Income-related surcharges (IRMAA)
for 2027 are also not yet published.

Social Security's 2027 cost-of-living adjustment is 3.6 percent. Because Part B premiums are deducted from the benefit,
a premium increase of the projected size would absorb a portion of that raise for most retirees.

## What you can decide before the Part B number arrives

1. **Run your drug list through the Plan Finder at Medicare.gov.** Plans change formularies every year. The $2,400 cap
   applies to covered drugs only; a drug that drops off your plan's formulary does not count toward it.
2. **Check that your physicians and hospital are in network** if you are in or considering Medicare Advantage.
   Networks change on January 1 and the notices are easy to miss.
3. **Compare total cost, not premium.** A $0-premium Advantage plan with a $700 deductible and high specialist copays
   can cost more than a modest-premium plan if you see specialists regularly.
4. **If you take an expensive brand-name drug, ask about the Medicare Prescription Payment Plan**, which spreads your
   out-of-pocket drug costs across the year instead of front-loading them in January.
5. **Hold your Medigap decision** until the Part B deductible is published if the choice between plans hinges on it.

## What to watch

The CMS fact sheet with 2027 Part B premiums, deductibles and IRMAA brackets, expected in mid-November. I will update
this article the day it posts.
