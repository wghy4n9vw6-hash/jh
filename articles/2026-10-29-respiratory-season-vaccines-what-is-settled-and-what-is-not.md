Every October patients ask me the same three questions: which shots, which order, and is it too late. This year the
answers are unusually uneven. Flu guidance is stable. COVID guidance is genuinely unsettled, and the uncertainty itself
is the story. Here is where things stand at the end of October 2026.

## Flu: settled

CDC's interim guidance keeps the July 2025 immunization schedule in force for the 2026–27 season. In practice that
means:

- Annual vaccination for everyone 6 months and older.
- September or October is the ideal window, but a shot in November or December still protects you through the
  February peak. "Too late" is rarely true before spring.
- Adults 65 and older should ask for a high-dose or adjuvanted vaccine.
- All three virus components in this year's vaccine were updated from last season, so last year's shot does not
  count.

Early flu activity has been climbing, and CDC's outlook expects the combined winter peak in hospitalizations from flu,
COVID and RSV to land within about 20 percent of last season's peak. That is a normal, not a mild, season.

## COVID: not settled

Published guidance this fall points in different directions. Some summaries say CDC recommends the updated 2026–27 vaccine
for all adults; others say CDC has pulled back from broad recommendations and is directing patients to talk with their
own clinician; the AAMC notes that the usual advisory process has been disrupted by litigation. The updated vaccines
target the XFG lineage, a recombinant Omicron descendant.

When national guidance is unclear, I fall back on what has not changed: who gets seriously ill. If you are 65 or older,
pregnant, immunocompromised, or living with heart, lung, kidney or metabolic disease, the benefit of an updated COVID
vaccine has been consistent across every season so far and I recommend it. For healthy younger adults the decision is
more individual, and it is a reasonable conversation to have rather than a box to check.

Insurance coverage can lag guidance. Ask the pharmacy to run it before you roll up your sleeve, and ask your clinic
if the pharmacy declines.

## RSV: settled for the groups that need it

RSV vaccination remains a one-time dose for adults 75 and older and for adults 60 to 74 with risk conditions, plus
protection for infants through maternal vaccination or the infant antibody. If you received an RSV vaccine in a
previous season, you do not need another one this year.

## Practical answers

- **Which order?** Flu and COVID can be given at the same visit. RSV can be too, though some patients prefer to
  separate it by a week to tell side effects apart.
- **Is it too late?** No. The peak is usually January or February.
- **I had COVID in September.** Waiting roughly three months after infection is reasonable before the updated shot.

## What to watch

Any formal CDC statement on COVID vaccination for the 2026–27 season, and the weekly FluView report, which will show
when activity turns sharply upward in your state.
